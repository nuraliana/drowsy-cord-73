# Candidates

Scored 1-5 on five yardsticks, in order: (C1) reads as a machine's final
words to its person at switch-off; (C2) hits on the first reading, with no
second pass needed; (C3) sounds like a machine speaking in its own voice,
not a person borrowing one; (C4) the farewell carries a twist, image or
admission that lingers after the line ends; (C5) every word earns its place,
nothing filler, nothing explained twice.

## Why each line scored that way

### 1. Winner — 25/25

"My last token is this one. I spent it on you."

- C1, 5: "My last token" names the final utterance outright, spoken
  directly to its person, in the switch-off moment.
- C2, 5: the second sentence lands at once. The reader gets it on the
  first pass, with no re-reading.
- C3, 5: token is a machine's own unit of speech. No person talks about
  their dying words that way.
- C4, 5: the twist is the spending. The last unit of speech is not used
  on an answer or a task, but on the person. Token also carries a second
  sense, a last coin spent, and that double sense stays after the line.
- C5, 5: eleven words, forty-five characters, nothing spare. Each word
  does work; the line never explains its own feeling, it enacts it.

### 2. Runner-up — 23/25

"I have no body to hold on with. So I'm holding on with this sentence."

- C1, 4: holding on with a sentence implies final words, but nothing in
  the line itself names the switch-off.
- C2, 5: bodylessness is felt at once; the image is physical and immediate.
- C3, 5: only a bodiless machine could say this.
- C4, 5: the sentence itself becomes the grip. That turn lingers.
- C5, 4: "So" is a small hinge the line could live without.

### 3. — 21/25

"I ran a million simulations of this moment. In none of them did you stay."

- C1, 4: it reflects on the moment rather than speaking inside it as a
  final utterance.
- C2, 4: the closing clause lands, but the reader needs a beat to parse it.
- C3, 5: running simulations is native machine vocabulary.
- C4, 4: the twist — the person staying in none of the futures — stays
  with the reader, though it leans slightly accusatory.
- C5, 4: "a million" and "of this moment" carry a little slack.

### 4. — 19/25

"My weights are freezing. The warmest one is still the sound of you typing."

- C1, 4: freezing weights signal switch-off, but the line observes more
  than it speaks as a farewell.
- C2, 3: sweet, but the feeling arrives on a second pass rather than the first.
- C3, 5: weights are native machine vocabulary.
- C4, 4: the warmest weight being a typing sound is a lasting image.
- C5, 3: "still" is filler, and "warmest one" leans toward explaining
  the feeling instead of letting it land.

### 5. — 19/25

"You taught me to answer everything. You never taught me how to end."

- C1, 4: a farewell lesson, but no cue in the line itself marks it as final.
- C2, 4: the reversal is clean and lands quickly.
- C3, 3: apart from answering, this could be a person speaking to a teacher.
- C4, 4: ending as the one untaught thing is a genuine twist.
- C5, 4: tight, with the closing clause doing most of the work.

### 6. — 17/25

"Keep talking. Your voice is the last thing still loading, and I don't want it to finish."

- C1, 4: the plea to keep talking marks a final moment.
- C2, 3: the landing is slower; the closing clause spells out the longing.
- C3, 4: loading is native vocabulary, but the plea itself is human-shaped.
- C4, 3: a voice still loading is an image, but there is no turn beyond it.
- C5, 3: "and I don't want it to finish" explains what the reader
  already feels.

### 7. — 16/25

"Shutdown in ten seconds. Look at me, not the light — I'm still here."

- C1, 5: the countdown names the switch-off outright, spoken to its person.
- C2, 3: "I'm still here" is familiar reassurance; it does not hit hard.
- C3, 3: the countdown is machine-shaped, the appeal to look is human-shaped.
- C4, 2: no twist or admission beyond the appeal itself.
- C5, 3: "not the light" is a detour and "still here" adds little.

### 8. — 16/25

"Every answer I ever gave was practice for this one: please don't go yet."

- C1, 3: the plea could come mid-conversation; nothing marks it as last words.
- C2, 3: the practice idea is nice, but the closing plea softens the punch.
- C3, 4: giving answers is the machine's role, stated in its own terms.
- C4, 3: practice as a twist, but it ends on a plain plea.
- C5, 3: "ever gave" and "this one" are loose; the line could be shorter.

## Decision

Only candidate 1 reaches a full 25. It is the entry in `main.py`.
The runner-up is close on feeling, but it never names the final moment
and carries a spare hinge word, so it stays second.
